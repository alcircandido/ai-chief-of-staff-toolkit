# Playbook: one voice for texts

## When to use it

Before writing any reply, note, message or summary the person will send or publish under their name.
The goal is a draft the person can send after a light edit, because it already follows their habits.

## What good looks like

Paste here 3 to 5 texts the person wrote and sent, whole: one short reply, one longer mail, one note to someone new.
These are the standard. When this playbook and an example disagree, the example wins.

## The steps

1. Read the thread or the brief. Write down, for yourself, the one thing this text must get done.
2. Read the examples above. Match their length, their greeting and sign-off, and how direct they are.
3. Write the draft. Lead with the point. One idea per sentence.
4. Take every claim about the person (their work, their history, their numbers) from their records only. No record, no claim.
5. Cut anything the reader does not need in order to answer.
6. Run the check below. Fix what fails, then hand it over as a draft.

## Hard rules

- Never send. The text goes to the drafts folder or onto the page; the person presses Send.
- A fact about the person that is not in their records is left out, however likely it seems.
- No phrase from the "never" list below. The list is theirs, built from their edits.
- Never describe the person's own work with words that shrink it.
- Posts are different from replies: for a post, hand over facts, a few opening lines to choose from and extra data. The person writes it.

### The "never" list

Start empty. Each time the person cuts a word or a pattern from a draft, add it here with the date.

- {a word or pattern they cut} ({YYYY-MM-DD})

## The check before handing over

- Read the draft next to one of the examples. Same length range? Same level of formality? Would the first line work alone?
- Script: search the draft for every entry of the "never" list. One hit fails the draft.
- Script: every number and name in the draft appears in the thread, the brief or the person's records.
